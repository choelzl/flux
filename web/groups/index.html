    Groups¶

    Groups (top bar) shows your teams: members, what membership gives, whose settings runs use, and each group's projects. Admins create groups in Admin › People › Groups (Server and admin).

    A group is like an organisation; a project is like one of its repositories, with its own members. Loops are linked into projects.

    A group's page¶

    The head: the group's name with ▾ (switch group, or Global), its facts ("12 members · 5 projects · open projects give Asker"), and You: level. The address keeps the group (#/groups/<id>, #/groups/global).

    Tab Address What it shows
    Overview #/groups/<id> Projects (title, address <group>/<name>, invite only or open · level, your level, e.g. "Asker + curate"); New project for members who may create; People; Your access (your level and what it gives); Agents summary for admins and managers
    Members #/groups/<id>/members Names and levels; the member grid for managers
    Agents #/groups/<id>/agents Admins and managers: the group's agent settings and own agents (A group's agents), and its Schedule (A group's schedule)
    Machines #/groups/<id>/machines Admins and managers: the group's machines; + Add machine, labels, Rename, Drain/Resume, Rotate keys, Revoke

    members, agents and machines cannot be project names. An older project with one of those names keeps it; that tab is then at #/groups/<id>?tab=members.

    Members¶

    Level Gives
    Member Nothing extra
    Maintainer curate, create
    Lead curate, manage, create
    • Group membership gives nothing on any loop. A loop is reached when its owner shares it, or links it into a project you are in (or one open to the group).
    • A Lead adds people (People; they join as Member), removes anyone but themselves (×), and gives levels and words up to their own. Admins give any.
    • Others see names and levels, read-only. Nobody leaves a group here.

    New project¶

    New project: a Title and a Name (letters, digits, -, _, at most 60; not members or agents; unique in the group regardless of case). Create project makes <group>/<name>, blank and invite only, with you as Lead.

    A project's page¶

    Address: #/groups/<group id>/<name> (global: #/groups/global/<name>). Old #/projects/<group>/<name> links still open it.

    Head: group ▾ / title ▾ (switch group or project; + New project), its access (Invite only, Group views, Open; global: Public or Private), Chat (Project chats), You: level, and Leave for members.

    Tabs: Overview, Files (Library, Skills), Knowledge, Variables, Settings (only for those who may change something). Files, Knowledge, Variables and Chat are for its own members and admins (below).

    Overview What it shows
    Loops · N Linked loops you may see: owner / name, state, design agent, last active; for your own, its place in the loop's project order ("1st of its 3"). + Link one of my loops with the link role; the cross unlinks (the loop stays). A global project lists only your loops (all for admins and managers), with whether each learns
    People · N Members and levels; Manage › opens Settings
    Library File count and size; Open ›
    Your access Your words and how you have them

    Words on a project: view, ask, edit, run (on every linked loop), then link (link your loops), curate (review knowledge, keep the library), manage (title, members, access; rename, archive, delete). Each implies view. Words only add up. A global project's members hold only curate or manage; its visibility gives view.

    Access for the group's members (set by the project's managers, the group's managers, admins):

    • Invite only (default): hidden until you are added.
    • Group views: members view it and its loops.
    • Open to the group: every member gets its Level for open (Viewer, Asker, Contributor or Maintainer).

    There is no joining; anyone may leave.

    Its contents are its members'¶

    A project's files (Library, Skills), knowledge, variables and chats are for its own members and admins only. A group member who is not on the project's list (via Group views, Open to the group, or curate/manage on the group) sees the project, its people and its loops, but not those tabs: "You see that this project exists. Ask its managers to add people." Group managers can add themselves.

    A linked loop reads the project's library, skills and knowledge only while its owner is a member who views it.

    Library¶

    Files › Library: papers, specs and notes for the project's loops. Same browser as a loop's Files: upload (Files, Folder, drop; a .zip is unpacked), Rename, Move, Delete.

    • Limit: the project's own, else the server's (both in Admin › People › Projects), else 8 GB and 100,000 files. Shown as "1.2 GB of 5 GB". Over-limit changes are refused.
    • Every linked loop reads it read-only at library/<name>@<group>/ (library/<name>@_/ for a global project). Archived projects are read by no loop.
    Who May
    Its members who view it (any word; everyone for a public global project) and admins -- never through its group browse and download
    edit, curate or manage on it as its member (a global project's members with curate or manage) upload, rename, move, delete, edit a text file
    manage as its member (its managers, admins) Hide from AI / Show to AI: the paths the agents of its loops will not see (under its place, library/<name>@<group>/drafts/)

    Archived: read-only. Every change is audited.

    Skills¶

    Files › Skills: the project's skills/ folder, a folder per skill with a SKILL.md (Skills). Same people and browser as the library. Linked loops get them read-only at skills/<name>@<group>/.

    Knowledge¶

    Short entries, each one insight, filed under topics and proven by designs of the project's loops.

    • Accepted entries reach linked loops at their next start, read with the library (Knowledge). Proposals, rejected and retired entries never do.
    • Entries come from people (+ Add), the collector, and project chats (Project chats); the last two go to To review.
    • A line shows use: "Read by 2 of 3 linked loops this week, 14 times".
    • Chips: All, one per topic, To review (curators), + Add.
    • A row: headline, two lines of analysis, topic, backing ("✓ maria · 2 designs", "+1" reinforced today).
    • Opened: the analysis (#142 links to the design), Designs (rank "top 1 of 61", day, source), Ideas ("in 3 loops' ideas · tried 2 · won 1"; each accepted entry is an idea in every linked loop), who accepted it, and the toolbar: Reinforce, Pin, Merge, Edit, Retire.
    Who May
    Its members who view it (and admins; never through its group) read the list; their own proposals, marked in review
    edit, curate or manage + Add an entry (topic, one-line insight, analysis citing #142, at least one design); Reinforce with another design; write the Topics
    curate as its member To review: edit, ✓ accept, ✕ reject, Merge into; Edit, Retire, Pin, Merge listed entries
    • Proof is enough with designs from 2 different loops (backed by N loops). A curator's ✓ on less proof ticks it ("✓ maria").
    • A curator's own entry goes straight in (on a global project another curator accepts it).
    • A proposal may say contradicts: headline.
    • Merge keeps one entry and folds others into it; Unmerge restores them.
    • Pin puts an entry first (also in what the agents read).
    • Stale: Flux marks entries whose evidence faded; ✓ retires, ✕ keeps. Nothing retires by itself.
    • Everything is kept and audited. Archived: read-only.

    The collector¶

    With a link's learning on (default in a group's project; in a global one a manager or admin turns it on, and the loop's owner may turn it off), Flux reads linked loops' best designs once they change and writes proposals. Nothing in the loop changes. Billed to the loop owner's FluxAgent as knowledge collector (Admin › Activity › Usage).

    It finds What happens
    The new designs work by an accepted entry's mechanism They reinforce it at once, no review: "+ … collected from nlu-gelu, reinforced"
    They work by the mechanism of a proposal in review They join its designs: the proposal stays in To review, now backed by that loop too
    A new insight A proposal in To review, "collected from nlu-gelu"
    They contradict an entry A proposal marked contradicts: its headline, and on the entry a mark like 1 of 4 loops disagrees until a curator rejects that proposal; opened, the entry lists those designs under Against
    An entry is superseded or shown wrong A proposal retire: its headline, with the reason: ✓ retires the entry, ✕ keeps it. Nothing is retired by itself

    Collect now (managers, admins) lists the linked loops with their state (top designs unread, nothing new, learning off, no measured designs, not linked, ...).

    • Untick loops to skip. Archived loops are listed unticked; their records are only read.
    • Read designs again, even those already read: reads the top 5 regardless.
    • One model call per loop, in the background (Collecting 2 of 7…); audited.
    • A loop whose owner is not a project member is listed its owner is not a member: on your model: read only on Collect now, on your model, if you can view the loop.
    • Compare loops: finds what ticked loops share; one call per topic (~10-20k tokens), on your model, billed as knowledge compare. Needs two loops.
    • Last collected: nlu-gelu · Oct 23 · 2 proposed, 1 reinforced; Runs lists every run.

    Suggestions¶

    Off until a curator turns them on (Settings › Suggestions). Suggest on a proposal asks a model for one move (accept, reject, merge, a better headline) with why; Apply does it. Never automatic. Runs on the pressing curator's FluxAgent. Also as each proposal arrives, on my model suggests automatically on that curator's model. Audited ("knowledge suggest"); usage as knowledge suggestions.

    Variables¶

    The project's environment variables: name, value, secret (stored encrypted, shown as secret · set), bin, Add. Names: letters, digits, _; Flux's own names (the sandbox's, PATH, HOME, model settings) are refused.

    Each linked loop reads them at its next start, in every process. Later wins:

    1. the server's (Admin › Runtime › Agents › All agents),
    2. the loop owner's (Account),
    3. each project, in the loop's order (the first project wins over later ones),

    4. the loop's own (Settings › Variables).

    5. Only while the project is unarchived and the owner is a member who views it.
