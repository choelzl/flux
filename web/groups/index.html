    Groups¶

    Groups, in the top bar between Loops and Admin, is every user's page for the teams they are in: who is in each group, what their membership gives them, whose settings their runs use, and the group's projects. Admins set groups up under Admin › Users › Groups (Server and admin); this page is where everyone else sees them.

    A tab per group¶

    The page has a tab for each of your groups and one for Global (with its number of projects), with no title above them (the bar says Groups). The tab you chose is in the address (#/groups/<id>, #/groups/global) and remembered in this browser; a project opens under its group's tab (A project's page).

    Under a group's tab:

    Part What it shows
    Projects The group's projects: title and about, address (<group>/<name>), invite only or open · level beside the title, and your level on it -- what the project gives you (Viewer, Asker, Contributor, Lead; Custom only for a mix of boxes), with your group's curate or manage said beside it ("Asker + curate"); its words on hover; "—" when you only see it. An invite-only project you are not added to is not listed. The address is one line, cut with an ellipsis when it is long (the whole on hover). Members who may create, and admins, get the New project form under the list
    You in group A few facts: your Level (its words on hover, the words), what it Gives in plain words (curate or manage every project of the group, create projects; loops only through the projects you are in or that open to the group), Open projects (the level they give, for its managers and admins to choose) and Credentials in one line (the server's settings through it, through another group, or your own)
    Members · N Who is in it: names and levels for everyone; the member grid (a box per word) for whoever manages the group

    On a phone You in comes first, then the projects and the members.

    Members¶

    A membership's level is Member, Maintainer (curate, create) or Lead (curate, manage, create): a group membership has no loop word. Being in a group gives nothing on any loop: a loop is reached when its owner shares it, or links it into a project you are in (or that opens to the group). A member who manages the group (a Lead) sees the members in the member grid, adds people with + Add people (they join as Member), removes anyone but themselves with ×, and gives levels and words up to their own; admins any. A member who may not manage the group sees the other members, their names and levels, read-only (never the words' table), and is told whom to ask. Nobody leaves a group from here.

    New project¶

    New project asks for a Title (free) and a Name, made from the title until you type one. The name is checked as you type: letters, digits, - and _, at most 60, and not one the group already has whatever its case. Create project makes <group>/<name>, blank and invite only, and you are its Lead (every word). Nothing is said under the form but an error, when there is one. A project's title opens its page.

    A project's page¶

    Think of a group as an organisation and a project as one of its repositories, with members of its own; loops are linked into it. Its page opens on the Groups page, under its group's tab (the tab lit; a global project under Global): #/groups/<group id>/<name>, #/groups/global/<name>. Every link Flux makes to it is this one. An old address, #/projects/<group>/<name>, still opens it (a link in an older notification, a bookmark) and is replaced by the new one.

    Under the group tabs:

    • Projects › its title ▾ -- Projects goes back to the group's projects; ▾ lists the group's other projects you see, with their loops, and + New project for whoever may make one. A pick opens the other project on the same tab when it has it.
    • Its head: the title, what the group's members get (🔒 Invite only, Group views, Open; a global project Public or 🔒 Private; its sentence on hover), You: your level (its words on hover), its address with a copy button, its about. Leave, a quiet button there, for its own members.
    • Its tabs: Overview, Library, Knowledge and Settings (only for whoever may change something there -- a Contributor's holds the topics alone; a plain member or a Viewer has none), each its own address: …/<name>/library, …/<name>/knowledge, …/<name>/settings.
    Overview What it shows
    Loops · N Each loop linked into it that you may see: owner / name, its state, when last active, and for your own its place in the loop's order of projects ("1st of its 3"). + Link one of my loops if you hold the link role (anyone on a global project that takes loops); × takes one of yours out (the loop stays). A global project lists only your own loops, every one to admins and its managers, with whether each learns from its runs. What linking gives is on the ⓘ
    People · N Its members, names and levels (their words on hover); Manage › opens Settings for whoever keeps them
    Library How many files it holds and their size; Open ›
    Your access Your words, and how you have them in a line: as a member, through the group, everyone on a public global project (what they give on its loops on hover)

    On a phone the Overview is one column, Loops first.

    Words: view, ask, edit, run -- what a member may do with every loop linked into it -- then link (link one's own loops in), curate (review what is learnt, keep the library) and manage (title, members, access; rename, archive, delete). Each implies view. A global project's members hold only curate or manage (Member, Maintainer, Lead): its visibility gives view. Sharing a linked loop stays its owner's. Words only add up: a project never takes one away.

    Who manages it: its managers, the group's managers (manage on the group) and admins, each giving words up to their own. They also set what the group's members get -- Invite only (private: hidden until you're added -- only its members, the group's curators and managers and admins see it; a new project's default), Group views (they view it and its loops) or Open to the group (every member gets its Level for open: the group's default, or one of its own -- Viewer, Asker, Contributor or Maintainer -- that level, nothing more). There is no joining; anyone may leave a project. Others see its people, names and levels, in the Overview.

    Library¶

    A project's Library is a folder of papers, specs and notes for its loops, browsed exactly as a loop's Files tab: the tree, the viewer (text in the editor, anything else to download), Add files and Add folder (or a drop; a single .zip is unpacked) into the folder open in the viewer, and Rename…, Move…, Delete… on the selected item. Its use is shown beside the room it has: a loop's, 100,000 files and 8 GB. Above it, which loops read it: every loop linked into the project reads it, read-only, at library/<name>@<group>/ of its own library (library/<name>@_/ for a global project), whoever runs it -- the loops you see are named, the others counted. An archived project's library is read by no loop, nor by a loop whose owner no longer views the project.

    Who May
    Whoever views the project (its members, its group as its access says, everyone for a global one) browse and download
    edit, curate or manage on it -- as its member, through its group (an open project's level; a membership's curate and manage reach every project of the group), a global project's members with curate or manage upload, rename, move, delete, edit a text file
    manage (its managers, the group's managers, admins) Hide from AI / Show to AI: the paths the agents of its loops will not see (under its place, library/<name>@<group>/drafts/)

    An archived project's library is read-only. Uploads, renames, moves, edits, deletes and hidden paths are on the audit trail.

    Knowledge¶

    A project's Knowledge is what its loops have taught: short entries, each one insight, filed under the project's topics and proven by designs -- results of loops, never a number typed in. The accepted entries reach the project's loops at their next start: their agents query them with flux knowledge (list, search "<words>", show <id>; see Knowledge), and a coding agent's brief says so in one line -- the project's place and its topics with their counts. Proposals, rejected and retired entries never reach a loop; a run reads the entries as its start saw them, and its log line says the version ("project knowledge silicon@hw-lab 3f2a… (5 entries)"). People add entries by hand, and the collector proposes them from the loops' best designs (below).

    The tab is a row of chips and one list. All and a chip per topic (with its count; the topic's question on hover) filter the list; To review, for its curators, holds the proposals; + Add is at the row's end. A row is an entry's headline with two muted lines of its analysis, its topic and how many designs back it at the right -- "✓ maria · 2 designs" when a curator ticked it, "+1" when it was reinforced today. A click opens it: the analysis at a reading width (a design named #142 in it is a link), its Designs (each a link to that design in its loop's Results, with its rank then -- "top 1 of 61" --, the day, how it came: "added by cy", "collected from nlu-gelu", later "from bob's chat in nlu-gelu"; one added later marked "+ … reinforced"), who accepted it, and Reinforce…, Edit and Retire for whoever may.

    Who May
    Whoever views the project read the list; their own proposals, marked in review
    edit, curate or manage + Add an entry -- a topic, the insight in one line, the analysis (why it works, what the top designs did differently, citing them as #142, the cost, where it does not hold, what is open) and at least one design of a loop of the project they view; Reinforce… an entry in the list with another design, at once, no review; write the Topics
    curate (its curators, the group's curators and managers through the cascade) To review: open a proposal to change its headline and analysis, ✓ accepts it, ✕ rejects it; Edit and Retire an entry in the list

    An entry's proof is enough with designs from 2 different loops. Until then it is a proposal; a curator's ✓ on one with less accepts it and ticks it ("✓ maria"), and the list says so. A curator's own entry goes straight in (ticked when its proof is short) -- on a global project another curator accepts it. A proposal may be marked contradicts: an entry's headline. Accepted, rejected and retired entries stay on the record; the author is told when a curator decides. An archived project's knowledge is read-only. Every change (topics, add, accept, reject, edit, retire, reinforce, a collector's run) is on the audit trail.

    The collector¶

    Flux reads a loop's best designs for each project it is linked into -- unarchived, with topics, the link's learning on (on by default in a group's project; in a global one only its managers or an admin turn it on, and the loop's owner may turn it off) -- and writes what they teach as proposals, outside the loop: no step of the loop, nothing in its prompts or its folders changes. It looks once the loop's top 5 designs (by the loop's own ranking, as "top 1 of 61" says it) hold more than 3 that it has not read for that project: the loop's front has mostly moved since the last look. A design is read once; a name given again by a later start is another design. The server looks when a start ends and, while loops run, every two minutes at the ones whose record changed, one look at a time in the background.

    A look is one call to the loop's own model -- its owner's FluxAgent settings for that loop, at their cost, about 6-12k tokens in and 3k at most out -- with the new top designs (their idea, their text or its diff against the loop's baseline, their numbers), the rest of the loop to compare, and the project's entries. It answers with at most six items, each checked before anything is written:

    It finds What happens
    The new designs work by an accepted entry's mechanism They reinforce it at once, no review: "+ … collected from nlu-gelu, reinforced"
    A new insight A proposal in To review, "collected from nlu-gelu"
    They contradict an entry A proposal marked contradicts: its headline
    An entry is superseded or shown wrong A proposal retire: its headline, with the reason: ✓ retires the entry, ✕ keeps it. Nothing is retired by itself

    Its rules are Flux's own: an insight answers a topic's question, in a one-line headline and an analysis of 80-200 words -- the mechanism, what the top designs did differently from the rest (citing them, #142), the cost, where it does not hold, what is open -- proven by the designs it names; no bare conclusions, vague tips or flag trivia, and nothing when the designs do not explain the mechanism and its limits. An item that does not hold (a topic or an entry that is not there, a design it was not shown, an analysis of the wrong length or citing none of its designs) is dropped and said on the audit. A call that fails reads nothing (the designs wait for a later look, not within the hour); a reply that is not the JSON asked counts as read. Curators see its last run in a quiet line under the list: Last collected: nlu-gelu · Oct 23 · 2 proposed, 1 reinforced (on hover: how many designs it read, how many items it dropped). Its calls count in Admin › Insights' usage as the loop's and its owner's, as knowledge collector under By agent (priced by the loop's FluxAgent token prices), and a failed one as a failed turn of its endpoint.

    Settings¶

    For its managers (and admins): Members & access first -- its members in the same grid as a group's: a level (the words), the Member mark, a box per word, ×; All members gives or takes a word from everyone; + Add people (they join as Member); and what the group's members get, with the Level for open. Then About (title and about), Name with Rename… -- the new name is checked as typed, the old address stops working (no redirect) and its members and its group's are told the new one; the page opens at the new address -- Archive… / Unarchive and Delete… (type its name; its loops stay, taken out of it). On a global project admins also choose its Visibility -- Public (everyone sees it, its library and knowledge) or Private (only its members and admins) -- and who links loops into it (everyone, or admins only; a private one's members only). Topics, for whoever may edit the project (a Contributor too, who sees only this card): a row a topic -- its id (lower-case letters, digits, - and _) and the question its entries answer -- edited in place, + Topic, ✕, Save; YAML shows the project's knowledge.yaml as written (pipelining: Where do pipeline stages pay off, and what do they cost?, a line a topic, comments kept). A topic renamed takes its entries with it; one that still holds an entry is not removed. A member who may change nothing there (a plain member) sees no Settings tab.

    Global¶

    Global lists the global projects you see: the public ones (everyone sees them, their library and knowledge) and the private ones you are a member of (nobody else but admins finds them). Their members curate or manage them; there is no curator group. Only admins make them: New global project under the list (admins only), Admin › Users › Projects, or flux project create _/<name> (public; --visibility private). Anyone may link a loop into a public one, a private one's members into it (admins only when its join rule says so); it gives nobody any right on the loop, shows you only your own loops in it (admins and its managers see every one; in a private one its members do not see each other either), and learns from a loop's runs only once an admin or one of its managers turns that on.

    In no group¶

    A user in no group sees what a group is, that an admin adds them to one, and the Global tab. Their own loops and those shared with them are under Loops as always.

    Admins¶

    An admin sees their own groups; Every group (admin), at the end of the tabs, adds the others, marked "not a member". In every group an admin sets any member's level in the grid, the group's Credentials (Server settings or Own settings) and its open level, adds and removes members, and makes projects. Every group in Admin › Groups opens Admin › Users › Groups.
