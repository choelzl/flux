    Groups¶

    Groups, in the top bar between Loops and Admin, is every user's page for the teams they are in: who is in each group, what their membership gives them, whose settings their runs use, and the group's projects. Admins set groups up under Admin › People › Groups (Server and admin); this page is where everyone else sees them.

    A tab per group¶

    The page has a tab for each of your groups and one for Global (with its number of projects), with no title above them (the bar says Groups). The tab you chose is in the address (#/groups/<id>, #/groups/global) and remembered in this browser; a project opens under its group's tab (A project's page).

    Under a group's tab:

    Part What it shows
    Projects The group's projects: title and about, address (<group>/<name>), invite only or open · level beside the title, and your level on it -- what the project gives you (Viewer, Asker, Contributor, Maintainer, Lead; Custom only for a mix of boxes), with your group's curate or manage said beside it ("Asker + curate"); its words on hover; "—" when you only see it. An invite-only project you are not added to is not listed. The address is one line, cut with an ellipsis when it is long (the whole on hover). Members who may create, and admins, get the New project form under the list
    You in group A few facts: your Level (its words on hover, the words), what it Gives in plain words (curate or manage every project of the group, create projects; loops only through the projects you are in or that open to the group), Open projects (the level they give, for its managers and admins to choose) and Credentials in one line (the server's settings through it, through another group, or your own)
    Members · N Who is in it: names and levels for everyone; the member grid (a box per word) for whoever manages the group

    On a phone You in comes first, then the projects and the members.

    Members¶

    A membership's level is Member, Maintainer (curate, create) or Lead (curate, manage, create): a group membership has no loop word. Being in a group gives nothing on any loop: a loop is reached when its owner shares it, or links it into a project you are in (or that opens to the group). A member who manages the group (a Lead) sees the members in the member grid, adds people with People (they join as Member), removes anyone but themselves with ×, and gives levels and words up to their own; admins any. A member who may not manage the group sees the other members, their names and levels, read-only (never the words' table), and is told whom to ask. Nobody leaves a group from here.

    New project¶

    New project asks for a Title (free) and a Name, made from the title until you type one. The name is checked as you type: letters, digits, - and _, at most 60, and not one the group already has whatever its case. Create project makes <group>/<name>, blank and invite only, and you are its Lead (every word). Nothing is said under the form but an error, when there is one. A project's title opens its page.

    A project's page¶

    Think of a group as an organisation and a project as one of its repositories, with members of its own; loops are linked into it. Its page opens on the Groups page, in its group (a global project in Global): #/groups/<group id>/<name>, #/groups/global/<name>. Every link Flux makes to it is this one. An old address, #/projects/<group>/<name>, still opens it (a link in an older notification, a bookmark) and is replaced by the new one.

    The group tabs are not drawn over it: its head is one row, then its tabs.

    • Its head: its group ▾ / its title ▾ -- the group's name goes back to the group's projects, its ▾ lists your other groups (and Global) and opens the one you pick on its projects; the title's ▾ lists the group's other projects you see, with their loops, and + New project for whoever may make one -- a pick opens the other project on the same tab when it has it; the title's address (<group>/<name>) on hover. Then what the group's members get (Invite only, with a lock, Group views, Open; a global project Public or Private, with a lock; its sentence on hover), and at the row's end Chat, which opens its conversations in Chat's page, on a new one (see Chat › Project chats: read by its members, asked by edit, curate or manage -- shown to its members alone), You: your level (its words on hover) and Leave, a quiet button, for its own members. Its about, when it has one, under the row. On a phone the row wraps: the group, the title and its access first, the buttons under them.
    • Its tabs: Overview, Files, Knowledge, Variables and Settings (only for whoever may change something there -- a Contributor's holds the topics alone; a plain member or a Viewer has none) -- Files, Knowledge, Variables and Chat for its own members (and admins) alone (Its contents are its members'), each its own address: …/<name>/library, …/<name>/knowledge, …/<name>/variables, …/<name>/settings. Files, as a loop's, has sub-tabs for the project's folders, Library and Skills (…/<name>/library, …/<name>/skills; …/<name>/files opens the first), each the same file browser. The tabs are drawn as a loop's page draws its own: underlined, and the sub-tabs on their own row under them, each with its count -- the library's files, the skills found ("Library 12 · Skills 3"). An empty folder says so inside the file list's card ("Nothing in the library yet.", "No skill yet."). At the sub-tabs' row's end, no row of its own: the library's use of its limit ("1.2 GB of 5 GB", Library shown) and, to whoever may change nothing in the folder shown, that it is read-only and why.
    Overview What it shows
    Loops · N Each loop linked into it that you may see: owner / name, its state and its design agent (as the Loops list says them, following them as they change), when last active, and for your own its place in the loop's order of projects ("1st of its 3"). + Link one of my loops if you hold the link role (anyone on a global project that takes loops); the cross takes one of yours out (the loop stays). A global project lists only your own loops, every one to admins and its managers, with whether each learns from its runs. What linking gives is on the ⓘ
    People · N Its members, names and levels (their words on hover); Manage › opens Settings for whoever keeps them
    Library How many files it holds and their size; Open ›
    Your access Your words, and how you have them in a line: as a member, through the group, everyone on a public global project (what they give on its loops on hover)

    On a phone the Overview is one column, Loops first.

    Words: view, ask, edit, run -- what a member may do with every loop linked into it -- then link (link one's own loops in), curate (review what is learnt, keep the library) and manage (title, members, access; rename, archive, delete). Each implies view. A global project's members hold only curate or manage (Member, Maintainer, Lead): its visibility gives view. Sharing a linked loop stays its owner's. Words only add up: a project never takes one away.

    Who manages it: its managers, the group's managers (manage on the group) and admins, each giving words up to their own. They also set what the group's members get -- Invite only (private: hidden until you're added -- only its members, the group's curators and managers and admins see it; a new project's default), Group views (they view it and its loops) or Open to the group (every member gets its Level for open: the group's default, or one of its own -- Viewer, Asker, Contributor or Maintainer -- that level, nothing more). There is no joining; anyone may leave a project. Others see its people, names and levels, in the Overview.

    Its contents are its members'¶

    A project's files (Library, Skills), its knowledge (topics, entries, proposals, the collector's runs), its variables and its chats are read by its own members alone -- what their level there says -- and admins. Its group gives none of them: a member of the group who is not on the project's member list -- through Group views, Open to the group at any level, or a membership's curate or manage -- sees the project, its people and the loops its access gives, and no Files, Knowledge or Chat: a link to them shows the project's head and "You see that this project exists. Ask its managers to add people.", and Chat's Projects tab does not list it. The group's managers still keep its members, so they may add themselves. A loop linked into the project reads its library, skills and knowledge only while its owner is a member who views it; its Chat, asked by someone who is not, answers without them; the collector proposes nothing from a loop whose owner is not. A global project's view is as its visibility says (public: everyone).

    Library¶

    Files › Library, a project's library, is a folder of papers, specs and notes for its loops, browsed exactly as a loop's Files tab: the tree, the viewer (text in the editor, anything else to download), Files and Folder (or a drop; a single .zip is unpacked) into the folder open in the viewer (the library's top when none is), and Rename, Move, Delete on the selected item. Its use is shown beside its limit -- "1.2 GB of 5 GB" at the end of the sub-tabs' row, its files the Library sub-tab's count (whose limit on hover): the project's own, else the server's for every project, both set by admins in Admin › People › Projects, else a loop's room, 8 GB, and 100,000 files. An upload, a part or an edit that would take it past its limit is refused, saying what it holds, may hold and what it adds. A library over its limit (an admin lowered it) keeps its files and says so in warn: it cannot grow, while a delete, a move or a smaller file in a file's place still go. Every loop linked into the project reads it, read-only, at library/<name>@<group>/ of its own library (library/<name>@_/ for a global project), whoever runs it -- a loop's Files tab shows it there; the Library tab no longer lists the loops that read it. An archived project's library is read by no loop, nor by a loop whose owner no longer views the project -- is not its member with a word --, nor by a loop's agent answering someone who may not read it.

    Who May
    Its members who view it (any word; everyone for a public global project) and admins -- never through its group browse and download
    edit, curate or manage on it as its member (a global project's members with curate or manage) upload, rename, move, delete, edit a text file
    manage as its member (its managers, admins) Hide from AI / Show to AI: the paths the agents of its loops will not see (under its place, library/<name>@<group>/drafts/)

    An archived project's library is read-only. Uploads, renames, moves, edits, deletes and hidden paths are on the audit trail.

    Skills¶

    Files › Skills is the project's skills/ folder, beside its library: a folder per skill, each with a SKILL.md -- front matter with name and description (when it applies), then the instructions -- and the files it uses (Skills). It is browsed and changed with the library's file browser, by the same people (upload, rename, move, delete, a text file edited in place), and lists the skills found, a folder that is no skill said with why. Every loop linked into the project gets them, read-only, at skills/<name>@<group>/, listed on its configurator's Skills card beside its own.

    Knowledge¶

    A project's Knowledge is what its loops have taught: short entries, each one insight, filed under the project's topics and proven by designs -- results of loops, never a number typed in. The accepted entries reach the project's loops at their next start: their agents query them with flux library (the list, flux library "<words>", flux library entry <id>; see Knowledge). Proposals, rejected and retired entries never reach a loop; a run reads the entries as its start saw them. People add entries by hand, the collector proposes them from the loops' best designs (below), and the project's chat proposes them too (Chat › Project chats): accepted there, an entry comes to To review ("from bob's chat"), never straight into the list.

    Under the list a quiet line says whether the loops use it: "Read by 2 of 3 linked loops this week, 14 times" -- how many of the loops it reaches ran flux library in the last 7 days, and how often. Archived loops count among the linked and are said apart ("Read by 2 of 4 linked loops (1 archived) this week"): they never run, so they never read it.

    The tab is a row of chips and one list. All and a chip per topic (with its count; the topic's question on hover) filter the list; To review, for its curators, holds the proposals; + Add is at the row's end. A row is an entry's headline with two muted lines of its analysis, its topic and how many designs back it at the right -- "✓ maria · 2 designs" when a curator ticked it, "+1" when it was reinforced today. A click opens it: the analysis at a reading width (a design named #142 in it is a link), its Designs (each a link to that design in its loop's Results, with its rank then -- "top 1 of 61" --, the day, how it came: "added by cy", "collected from nlu-gelu", later "from bob's chat in nlu-gelu"; one added later marked "+ … reinforced"), its Ideas -- "in 3 loops' ideas · tried 2 · won 1": each linked loop gets an accepted entry as one idea in its ideas notebook, scored and queued like its own (tried: a design of that loop followed it; won: one measured a win; see Knowledge) --, who accepted it, and its tools for whoever may, one toolbar of icons (each named on hover): Reinforce (a shield), Pin (a pin, pressed while the entry is pinned), Merge (two paths joining), Edit (the pencil) and Retire (an archive box: put away, still asking first).

    Who May
    Its members who view it (and admins; never through its group) read the list; their own proposals, marked in review
    edit, curate or manage + Add an entry -- a topic, the insight in one line, the analysis (why it works, what the top designs did differently, citing them as #142, the cost, where it does not hold, what is open) and at least one design of a loop of the project they view; Reinforce an entry in the list with another design, at once, no review; write the Topics
    curate as its member (its curators; a group's curators and managers only once they are its members) To review: open a proposal to change its headline and analysis, ✓ accepts it, ✕ rejects it, Merge into an entry in the list; Edit, Retire, Pin and Merge an entry in the list

    An entry's proof is enough with designs from 2 different loops: every row says backed by N loops, however many designs each gave, and opened it lists those designs loop by loop. The list leads with the pinned entries, then the best backed. A proposal always waits for a curator; a curator's ✓ on one with less proof accepts it and ticks it ("✓ maria"), and the list says so. A curator's own entry goes straight in (ticked when its proof is short) -- on a global project another curator accepts it. A proposal may be marked contradicts: an entry's headline. Accepted, rejected and retired entries stay on the record; the author is told when a curator decides. An archived project's knowledge is read-only. Every change (topics, add, accept, reject, edit, retire, reinforce, merge, pin, a collector's run) is on the audit trail.

    Merge joins entries of one topic that say the same thing: in the dialog you choose the entry to Keep, tick the others to merge into it, and edit its headline and analysis. The others' designs back the kept entry, each with its source; the merged entries leave the list and review and stay as its history -- opened, Merged lists them ("#12 headline · in review · merged by maria") with Unmerge, which puts them back as they were (the kept entry's text too, unless someone changed it since). A proposal that says the same as an entry in the list is merged into it from To review (Merge into); an entry in the list merges only into another in the list. Pin puts an entry first in the list, tagged pinned, and first in its topic in flux library and first among the entries a flux library "<words>" matches, the best backed after them -- an order only: loops still read knowledge by asking for it.

    Stale. Flux marks an entry in the list stale for its curators when the evidence no longer backs it: it has gone a long while without reinforcement or use, or its ideas were tried in several loops and won in none. The row says stale, a Stale chip lists them, and the mark says why with ✓, which retires the entry, and ✕, which keeps it. Nothing is retired by itself, and a mark whose reason no longer holds goes by itself.

    The collector¶

    With the link's learning on (on by default in a group's project; in a global one only its managers or an admin turn it on, and the loop's owner may turn it off), Flux reads the best designs of the loops linked into a project with topics, once they have changed, and writes what they teach as proposals, outside the loop: nothing in the loop changes. A look is billed to the loop owner's FluxAgent settings for that loop, as knowledge collector in Admin › Activity › Usage.

    What it can find:

    It finds What happens
    The new designs work by an accepted entry's mechanism They reinforce it at once, no review: "+ … collected from nlu-gelu, reinforced"
    They work by the mechanism of a proposal in review They join its designs: the proposal stays in To review, now backed by that loop too
    A new insight A proposal in To review, "collected from nlu-gelu"
    They contradict an entry A proposal marked contradicts: its headline, and on the entry a mark like 1 of 4 loops disagrees until a curator rejects that proposal; opened, the entry lists those designs under Against
    An entry is superseded or shown wrong A proposal retire: its headline, with the reason: ✓ retires the entry, ✕ keeps it. Nothing is retired by itself

    Collect now, beside Runs on the line under the list, is for the project's managers (and admins). It opens a dialog listing the linked loops, each with its state: how many top designs are unread, nothing new, or why no look can run (learning off, no measured designs, not linked, its owner's model missing...). Untick the loops to leave out. An archived loop is listed too, marked archived and unticked: tick it to read its top designs. Its record is only read -- the loop is never started, unarchived or changed. Flux also reads an archived loop by itself when any of its top designs is unread -- once it is archived, and again when the project's topics change -- rather than waiting for more than 3, which an archived loop will never have; after that it says nothing new. Without Read designs again, even those already read, a look reads only what is unread; with it, a look reads the loop's top 5 designs whatever was read before. Either way a look is one model call per loop, billed to the loop's owner, and what the project holds already is reinforced or left out, not proposed twice. Looks run one at a time in the background; the button says how far it is (Collecting 2 of 7…) and the press is on the audit trail, with its loops.

    A loop whose owner is not a member of the project (they left it, or linked the loop without joining) is still collected, but never on its owner's model: a look sends the project's topics and entries to the model, and those are for the project's members only. So such a loop is listed its owner is not a member: on your model and read only when you press Collect now, on your own model and billed to you -- if you can view the loop (it is shared with you, your level in the project gives it, or you are an admin). If you cannot, it says so and stays unticked: its owner joins the project or shares the loop with you. Flux never looks at such a loop by itself.

    Compare loops in the same dialog looks for what several of the ticked loops share, after the looks: one model call per topic, roughly 10-20k tokens each, on your own model and billed to you, as knowledge compare in usage; the dialog says the rough total first. It needs two ticked loops you view and a model of your own, and it never runs by itself. Its findings are proposals "compared by" you, backed by the loops they cite.

    Curators see the last run in a quiet line under the list: Last collected: nlu-gelu · Oct 23 · 2 proposed, 1 reinforced; Runs at its end opens Collector runs, every run newest first: its loop, when, what it proposed, reinforced or would retire, what was dropped and why, or why it failed. A line under the chips counts what became of the entries (7 accepted · 0 to review · 1 merged · 1 dropped): a proposal is one entry only once a curator accepts it.

    Suggestions¶

    Off until a curator turns them on in Settings › Suggestions. Then each proposal in To review has Suggest (opened): a model reads it against the entries in the list and suggests one thing -- accept, reject (for a retire proposal: retire or keep the entry), merge into an entry of its topic, or a better headline -- with a paragraph of why. The suggestion sits on the proposal's card, with the model and whose model it ran on; Apply does it as you could by hand (✓, ✕, the merge, or the headline changed with the proposal still in review), or you ignore it. Nothing is ever applied by itself. It runs on the FluxAgent settings of the curator who presses Suggest. Also as each proposal arrives, on my model asks for one as each proposal comes in (by hand or from the collector), on the model of the curator who ticked it, named under the setting. A suggestion reads the project's knowledge only, never a loop's or a library's files. Each call is on the audit ("knowledge suggest") and in Admin › Activity › Usage as that curator's, knowledge suggestions in its Agent table.

    Variables¶

    Variables holds the project's environment variables, in the editor a loop's and your account's use: a row a variable -- its name, its value (a secret's shown as secret · set: its value is stored encrypted and never shown or answered again; set it anew to change it) -- the bin to remove one, and Name, Value, secret, Add under them. Names are checked as everywhere: letters, digits and _; the sandbox's and Flux's own (FLUX_SANDBOX…, PATH, HOME, the model settings, ...) are refused.

    Every loop linked into the project reads them at its next start, in each of its processes (a run, a check, its Chat's and its agents' turns), in this order, each over the one before:

    1. the server's (Admin › Runtime › Agents › All agents),
    2. the loop owner's (Account),
    3. each project the loop is linked into, in the loop's order -- its first project wins over the later ones,
    4. the loop's own (Settings › Variables), which always win.

    A project counts as its library does: unarchived, and while the loop's owner is its member who views it; a loop's agent answering someone else (its Chat) reads only the projects they may read too. A loop in no project reads what it always did. A change -- set, removed, a loop linked or unlinked, the order changed -- reaches a loop at its next start, never a run already going. The loop's Settings › Variables lists them under its own, *from project

    / *, a plain value shown to whoever may read the project's variables (else the name alone), one that something above sets marked **overridden**.
