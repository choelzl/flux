    Results¶

    A loop's Results tab holds every design measured across all its starts. The decision is said in a line above three views: Designs, Graphs and Ideas. The library's digests are under Files › Library (an old link to Results › Library leads there).

    Designs¶

    One card of the designs, in five tabs, each with its count -- each status led by the mark its STATUS column draws, in its tone (a check, the reject mark, a cross, a ring; All only its word):

    • All: the designs with numbers that count -- the accepted and the rejected, the decision first. The Graphs draw these only.
    • Accepted: checked and measured, every limit met at the stage that judges it.
    • Rejected: checked and measured, but a number misses a limit or a stage's cutoff; or rejected by a person or a critic (a pill under its name: "rejected by bob", "rejected by the critic"; a select critic's says what it rejected: "rejected by the critic (final)" for the design itself, "rejected by the critic (this pick)" for a pick that counts again once its numbers change). A rejected design keeps its row (Restore undoes it), and no graph draws it, not even faint: its numbers may be wrong. A design that went no further than an early stage -- a cutoff cut it, an estimate skipped the next stage, or its pass ended (or the run stopped) before it climbed -- is here too, with a pill stopped at screen: its numbers are that stage's, drawn muted and italic, early, not final.
    • Failed: a check refused it, or a measurement gave nothing (refused, failed, no numbers), or it was measured again and refused. No measurement columns: its checks (one pill, below: where it failed, ✗ gate, what failed heading its tooltip -- "check failed · gate: ..."), the failure reason (its words alone), when.
    • Pending: designs still to be measured: checked and not measured yet ("its pass ended before a stage measured it", "not measured yet: its pass is still running", "the run stopped before a stage measured it"), measured while their pass goes on and a later stage judges them (their numbers so far), and those asked to be measured again (re-measure pending, their old numbers muted). Once measured through, a design leaves Pending for Accepted or Rejected -- a refusal of an earlier re-measure no longer holds it.

    A design measured before a change to how its stage is measured (its command, how its numbers are read, the params it takes, the files it reads or its tools: When a measurement still counts) stays in its tab with its numbers, muted, a small "measured before a change" mark after its name. The loop does not count those numbers, so no graph, ranking, pool or best draws them, and its detail says Not counted today. The Overview's card offers Accept as current, Leave as stale and Re-measure.

    Checks (Failed and Pending) is one pill, one line high: the deepest stage the design went through, in the flow's order (build, quick check, gate, admit, then the measuring stages), with its latest outcome -- ✓ gate for a design waiting for its measurement, ✗ esl for one whose esl measurement failed. Hover it for every stage's latest outcome. Each stage's latest attempt is the design's state there: a gate that passed after it failed is passed; a stage measured through after it gave nothing is passed; a build that failed and was built later is passed (the gate that passed after it ran on what built). Which tab a design is in, Re-Evaluate's boxes (a box per tab) and the Overview's counts read the same.

    A design's standing is its latest attempt, in the flow's order. When a newer attempt fails at a stage -- the gate refusing it after you changed the tests and re-evaluated, a stage that measured it before giving nothing now -- every stage after it from the older attempts is superseded: its old numbers are not the design's any more. It shows ✗ gate, not the old ✓ area; it is in Failed, with no numbers in the table, the Graphs, the ranking, the Pareto, the Overview or the Loops list's counts, and the loop itself no longer reads them (its frontier, who climbs, its decision). A design checked again and still being measured (a re-evaluation under way) is Pending with only what this attempt measured so far. The old attempts stay in the design's detail, folded under their stage, marked superseded ("the gate failed after it").

    Only an attempt of the design supersedes. A pass that tries to improve a design and makes no new one (its agent wrote the same design again, failed, ran out of time) is no attempt of the design it started from: the refusal is on the record as "no design", in Live and the log, and the design keeps its numbers -- and stays the decision if it was one. A record from before this rule keeps such a refusal under the design's own name, as a failed gate; it is passed over the same way.

    The measurements listed are the current ones. The table's columns, Settings › Preferences' Measurements, the Graphs' pickers, the Overview and Compare list what the designs' current attempts measured, and what the document names (its objectives, cutoffs and stages' metrics:). Change your scripts' metrics (or rename a stage) and re-evaluate: a metric only the old attempts carry -- superseded, set aside for a re-measure that has not measured them yet, or at a stage the document no longer has -- leaves the lists, as soon as anything is measured again. A design still waiting for its re-measure shows its old numbers under the columns that are still current; its detail keeps every number it had. A hidden or main measurement saved for a metric that left counts for nothing and comes back if the metric does. With every design set aside and none measured since, the lists are what the record holds.

    Results opens on Accepted; the browser remembers the tab you choose; the address names it (…/results?tab=failed), and a link to a design opens it in its tab.

    A page at a time. The table reads the rows it shows: scrolling to its end reads the next page (a spinner and "32 of 300" in the last row meanwhile). Choosing another tab or header reads the first page again in that order. Rows do not reshuffle while you scroll a running loop's results: the rows loaded are read again in place, the scroll where it was. A design opened from a graph or a link is brought into sight. The tab counts, the decision line and Compare work with every design, loaded or not.

    Sorting. A header click sorts the table; the arrow turns at once, and the rows shown stay, dimmed under a small spinner, until the new order comes.

    The table's own buttons are icons at the end of the tabs' row, each saying what it does on hover: the eye (hidden columns, how many beside it), the braces { } (inspect one dictionary), % (relative values), the star (the loop's ranking) and the two columns (compare, 1/2 once a design is ticked). A pressed one is tinted.

    • Order: the table opens in the loop's ranking (the star pressed and filled), as the Overview's Best 3: the decision, then the loop's rank, then the objective, best first. Click a header to sort by that column's values, the objective's and the limits' best first (a minimized one smallest first), designs with equal values in the ranking; click again for the other way, a third time (or the star) for the ranking. A design with no value in that column ("—", or a value that is not a number) is listed last whichever way.

    • Relative (%): numbers as they are, or, pressed, as percent change from the latest baseline (without one, from the accepted designs' 90th percentile, the 10th for a minimized objective, the median for a metric no objective ranks). Hover a cell for the value and its reference.

    • Numbers from an earlier stage: when stages measure different things (one timing, one area), a design's row shows each metric's deepest measurement; a number carried from an earlier stage is muted, and hovering it says which ("· from timing"). The design's detail writes that stage under the number.
    • Hidden columns (the eye): the columns hidden in Settings › Preferences shown again, dimmed, while it is pressed.
    • Dictionary metrics: a dictionary's column is its own name, its aggregate (its own _a, else the mean, when no objective says another: one no objective names is folded like any other). Its entries are no columns of their own: untick the dictionary's row in Settings › Preferences and it leaves the table whole -- no entry takes its place; tick one of its entries there (open the row with ▸) and that entry is a column after the dictionary's, read for it. To look into one, press { } among the table's icons ("Inspect one dictionary"; there only when the loop has dictionaries) and pick it in its menu (each with its entries' count): the table then shows that dictionary's entries alone as its columns -- cases, or the measurements under that metric -- under its name, every other measurement out of the way, sortable like any column. The icon stays pressed and a chip beside the icons names the dictionary ({ } timings ✕); ✕, or None at the end of the menu, brings the table back. The columns change at once, a spinner in each row while the entries are read; a dictionary hovered in the menu is read before you pick it, and one read already is drawn again without a read until the results change. A table of many rows and columns draws the rows in sight as you scroll. One dictionary at a time; your browser keeps the pick for the loop. A missing or empty entry stays unmeasured, never zero. Graphs charts a dictionary as its aggregate or per entry. {} raw has every number.
    • Compare (the two columns): tick two designs to see every number at every stage, the change (green where better, red where worse; a metric no objective ranks is not coloured), and their sources' difference.
    • A design opens with what it does not meet, its numbers at each stage, its idea and rationale, the rundown its design agent gave of it (Rationale: its key points, what it changed from the design it started from and why, "in its design agent's own words"; none for a model step's design or an agent that cannot resume), its checks -- one row per stage, in the flow's order: its latest attempt (outcome, time, output); the attempts it superseded folded under it (2 earlier attempts, a chevron before it, muted: the record keeps every one); a build said passed by the gate after it ("the gate passed after it, on what built": a build that passes is not recorded on its own); a stage that only ever measured it is in the numbers, not a row; a stage a newer attempt superseded is a muted superseded row ("the gate failed after it: not the design's now"), its attempts folded under it; its pass opens in Live with each check's log -- and its source; notes pinned on it from Chat, and Reference for comparison when it is marked so (for reading only: not the baseline the numbers compare with). The address names the open design (…/results?design=NAME): a link or a reload opens it.

    Checked, not measured¶

    A design only its checks judged opens the same detail as a measured one, with Not measured and why at its top: the check that refused it, a measurement tried that gave nothing ("its screen measurement was refused: estimated to fail"), or what it waits for. Check and measure again (its owner, with a reason) has it checked and measured at every stage: by the running loop before its next pass, or at rest by a run of its own; it waits in Pending until then. flux loop show NAME says the same reason ("not measured: …").

    Probed by agents¶

    A draft a design agent probed in its turn and did not hand in is listed, unverified -- never in the table, the graphs or the decision. Under Rejected and Failed one chip says how many ("+3 probed by agents"); it opens them: each by its name (#p3) and its reason ("not verified by the loop — probed by the agent only", or "failed the agent's own gate probe" with the check that failed). Its agent's numbers are marked unverified. Evaluate (the owner or an editor; audited) has the loop check, measure and choose it as a design of its own; it then has a number and the loop's verdict, and leaves the chip.

    Reject a design¶

    Reject on a design (its owner, with a reason) marks it: it stays shown but is never chosen. Tick its numbers to have them measured again or cleared; at rest, Also hide the design takes it out of the results, listed under Invalidated by hand, where *Re-measure checks and measures those designs again in one run, and Restore counts them again. A critic's rejection reads the same way; its detail names the critic (its agent), what it rejected (the design itself, final; or this pick, which lapses when its numbers change) and links Show the critic's reasoning: its critique in Live. Restore on a rejected design undoes every rejection standing on it at one press (a person's, a critic's, a refused re-measure); the loop, running, counts it again at its next decision. A design whose re-measure was refused offers Check and measure again beside Restore. The detail says when a re-measure happens: before the next pass (the loop running), by a run of its own (at rest: Live shows it), at the next start (Re-Evaluate's), or when a re-evaluation runs (Invalidate's).

    A press (Restore, Reject, Check and measure again) reads the results again in place: the same table, its tab, order and scroll, the design open kept, the row and the tabs' counts drawn anew.

    Star your key designs¶

    Star, the first tool of a design's head (before Reject), marks it as one of your key designs: a bookmark for the loop, several at once. The loop's owner and its editors star and unstar, while it runs or at rest; everyone who may read the loop sees the stars. Each press is kept on the loop's record with who and when (Admin › Audit lists it too). The tool is an outline star; starred, it is filled in Flux's orange, its tooltip says who starred it.

    The decision and a hand star are drawn apart: the decision is the loop's pick, a filled orange trophy after its name; a design you starred has a plain open orange star -- the same orange, a person's mark -- after its name in the table and in the design's head (a design both decided and starred has the trophy, then the star). Both sit on the text line at the same size. The table lists every design, starred or not: the star in a row is how you find it. The Graphs draw a starred design's point itself in Flux's orange (the legend says Starred); the decision's ring and the selected design's diamond stay as they are. The Overview's charts do not: there a starred design is a point like any other.

    A star changes nothing the loop does: its decision, critics, pool and passes never read it. flux loop starred lists each starred design with who starred it, when, and its numbers.

    Share a design¶

    Share, after the Star in a design's head, makes a read-only link to that one design, for people signed in to this Flux or for anyone with the link, for 1 to 90 days (never expires: signed-in only). It needs the Share right on the loop (its owner has it); without it there is no tool. The dialog lists the design's links, each with Copy and Revoke; Account › Shared links lists them all.

    The link's page is live (an update to the design shows): the design's short name and the loop's, its standing (accepted, pending, failed, rejected) and the decision's trophy, its Measurements at each stage, its point Among the designs -- the objective's two numbers, this design in Flux's orange and every other design an unnamed grey dot -- its idea, its rationale (its design agent's rundown) and its Checks' outcomes. Never in a link: its code, a check's output, notes, people's rejection reasons, other designs' names, the loop, the library, and nothing the loop hides from its agents. Revoked, expired, the right lost or the design gone, the link shows This link does not open (Share says the rest).

    Invalidate, re-evaluate¶

    After a check or a measurement script changed, the loop's Settings › Maintenance has three tasks for the record:

    • Invalidate all (this loop only, at rest, after a confirmation; never from Admin › Maintenance) sets every design on record aside: each stays listed in Pending with its old numbers, marked re-measure pending and never chosen. Nothing is measured again yet: above the table (and on the Overview's designs tile) it reads "40 of 40 set aside since …: re-measure pending until a re-evaluation runs". Running it again adds only designs recorded since.

    • Re-Evaluate asks the loop's next start: after its baseline, that start checks and measures again the designs of the Results tabs you tick, then its passes go on. Its dialog has a box per tab, each with its count as the tabs say it ("Rejected · 143"), and Rerun baseline; above them it says what the choice does, counted ("Re-checks and re-measures 12 accepted and 143 rejected designs at the next start, after its baseline"). One box at least:

      • Accepted, Rejected, Failed, Pending: the designs in that tab now, and no other. Pending is ticked by default -- those waiting for a stage (checked, not measured) and those set aside (re-measure pending): what Invalidate all set aside is measured only with Pending ticked. A design rejected by hand is in Rejected; measured again, its rejection stands. All four ticked is every design on record, invalidated first.
      • Rerun baseline: pass 0, first (a baseline on record otherwise stands).

    A design already re-measure pending (a rejection asked for it) is measured at the start whatever is ticked, as at any start. Until that start the task's row and the Overview say what was asked ("the baseline measured again, then the failed and pending designs re-evaluated") with Cancel; it can be asked while the loop runs. Each design's new numbers replace the old as it is measured: "re-measured 12 of 40 since the invalidation at …". A stop keeps every design measured so far: "paused: 12 of 40 done; continues at the next start" -- the next start measures only the designs still pending, the one a stop cut short included. A design today's checks refuse goes to Failed with why (check failed), until a later re-measure measures it through. - A record invalidated by a Flux of before Oct 22, 2026, once brought up to date, lists the rows its old mark hid under Invalidated by hand ("a re-evaluation before Oct 22, 2026 set these rows aside"), restored from there like any.

    Graphs¶

    • Pareto front: any two metrics, by default at the stage the loop decides on: the designs it decides among (its pool) drawn, every other design faint at the numbers it has, its point saying why it is not in the pool in Live's Choose words ("measured on screen only: its pass ended before it climbed to full", "cut on screen: …", "the same design as …", a stage the document no longer has); picking a stage draws that stage's numbers instead. The legend's faint key says how many of them beat the decision on the first objective ("faint: not in the pool (3 better than the decision)"). Accepted and failed coloured, the decision ringed, the front joined. Focus fits the axes to the feasible front and fades every point not on it (still there, still hoverable).
    • Improvement by design: the best so far for each metric chosen, design after design, each chart titled by its best and its change from the baseline ("best cycles (-12.9%)"; its value with no baseline). The best so far counts the designs the loop decides among (its pool), as the loop decides; every other design is drawn faint, its point saying why it is not in the pool. Baselines are gray reference lines. Focus keeps the metric's front -- the designs that set a new best, the corners of the best-so-far line -- as they are and fades every other point; the axes stay as they were. It is offered while a metric with a direction is charted (an extra number has no best); with Accepted only on as well, the accepted designs alone, those that set no new best faded.
    • The selected design is one for the page: a point clicked in any graph, a row opened in the table, or the design the address names. Every graph that plots it marks it the same way at once -- a diamond in Flux's orange (the logo's), larger than a point, over every other mark; on the Pareto front faint orange lines from it to both axes -- and its row in the table has an orange edge. A graph without it draws nothing more. Clicking it again, or Escape, clears it. In the Overview, a point clicked in a Best chart is marked in its other charts and on the Pareto front the same way (a Pareto point there still opens Graphs). The selection is the page's alone: nothing of the loop or its results changes.
    • Fullscreen (⛶) in each graph's card (here and the Overview's Best and Pareto front): the card's controls and graph in the fullscreen viewer, the graph drawn again at the larger size; Escape or ✕ closes it.

    Which way is better is an objective's alone: its direction, or its goal (>= 300, <= 0.5). A measurement no objective names (an extra number, a dictionary's mean) has none -- it is never guessed from its name: its graph draws its designs with no best so far and no best line ("not an objective"); a Pareto front with it on an axis draws the points and no front; its change in Compare is not coloured. Sorting its column works both ways.

    Both graphs draw the designs of All (accepted and rejected; a failed or pending design has no numbers that count). A point says its design's standing in the table's words, from the same tab: a design is pending on a graph only when Pending lists it. One that went no further than an early stage is drawn hollow and its tooltip says stopped at screen and why ("went no further than screen: its pass ended without it climbing"), as its row does -- never "pending" or what it waited for. Each graph's controls end with three chips: Stage: deepest ▾ (a menu: the stage whose numbers are drawn), Accepted only (a check) and Focus (a crosshair) -- icon chips, pressed while on, their names in their tooltips. Accepted only draws only the designs the Accepted tab lists; the baselines' lines stay. Accepted only and Focus are each one choice for every graph of the loop -- turned on in one graph, on in the other and in the Overview's Best and Pareto front -- kept by your browser for that loop, not saved with it: another viewer has their own. (A loop whose Pareto Focus was saved with it before is where a browser with no choice of its own starts.)

    Charting another metric or a dictionary per entry reads its points; the chart waits under a grey placeholder meanwhile.

    The other choices (metrics, axes, stage, scope) are saved with the loop.

    Ideas¶

    The loop's ideas notebook: each idea a model or agent proposed, and the trials that evaluated it, a page at a time as you scroll (each idea with the later ones of its title and those merged into it). Each idea has one state, drawn as a pill whose meaning shows on hover:

    State Pill Meaning
    proposed grey ring Not tested yet: no design has followed it.
    testing turning blue ring A design that follows it is being checked or measured now.
    checked green ring A design that follows it passed its checks but has no numbers yet.
    measured green check A design that follows it has numbers. That is evidence, not proof that it helped.
    refused red cross Every design that tested it was refused by the checks.
    interrupted orange bang Its last test stopped before an outcome (the loop was stopped).

    "Refused" says only that no try passed. Open an idea's evidence and it lists one row per design that followed it: the design, the furthest stage it reached, its outcome there and its numbers there. A design's other tries (a build that failed, a gate refusal it was repaired from, another stage) fold into its row ("2 earlier tries"), each with its stage, outcome, reason and when. There is no pass column: a pass's number starts again with each run.

    Which try speaks for a design: of the stages it has an outcome at that says something (anything but inconclusive, below), the furthest -- the check stages (build, gate, ...) below the measuring ones, each in the order the design reached them -- and there a measurement before a passed check before a refusal by tests. A design that failed to build twice, was refused by the gate, then passed it and measured a win at the next stage is one win: its earlier failures do not count against it. A design refused by its tests at its furthest stage is "tests refused"; one with only inconclusive tries is inconclusive. What each design says of the idea is under its outcome, in a word, with why on hover (and each folded try's the same way):

    Outcome Meaning
    win Measured better than the standing design at that stage on some objective, by more than the noise, and worse on none.
    no gain Measured within the noise of the standing design on every objective.
    loss Measured worse beyond the noise, and better on none.
    tests refused A check ran and refused the design: failed tests, or a critic. This counts against the idea.
    checked Passed a check with no numbers yet, or a prototype that passes every input and misses the cost goal.
    inconclusive Says nothing about the idea: it did not build, the check did not run, the coding agent or a tool failed (a quota, a time limit, a permission), or the run stopped.

    A win, a loss or no gain is judged against the objective's noise (its tie and the spread of repeated measurements). An idea with inconclusive designs says how many under its evidence ("inconclusive ×4").

    Each idea has a score, under its state: a number and a word for the kind of evidence it has ("78.7 · repeated win"), with what makes it on hover. The list is ordered by it, the best first; the select in the State column's head lists them Newest first or by Number (idea 1 first) instead. The score moves only when the evidence does: measured wins raise it, losses and refusals by tests lower it, inconclusive designs do not count, and an untested idea slowly loses ground as it ages. The word is the evidence's kind, best first: repeated win, single win, untested with a priority, checked, inconclusive only, untested, no gain, stale, near a refused idea, loss, refused by tests.

    The bar above the list filters it by state: All or one state, each with its count across the whole notebook. A state with no ideas is not shown (unless it is the one chosen); All and Queued always are. Marked and Deleted appear the same way, once an idea is marked or deleted. Queued, last and in blue with the queue's icon (dimmed while empty), lists the ideas queued for testing in their order: the first is the one the next free pass tests. A loop at work does not reorder the list under you.

    Queue an idea and each of the next passes builds a design that follows the oldest queued idea, until a design that follows it has an outcome that says something of it; then the next one in line. A design with only inconclusive tries (it did not build, the check did not run, the coding agent or a tool failed, the run stopped) keeps the idea queued; once such designs came of three passes (several designs of one pass are one pass) the idea leaves the queue, and Live says so: fix the setup and queue it again. Each row's queue button (the lines with a plus) is a toggle: press it to queue the idea, and press it again, now tinted blue, to take it off the queue. Its tooltip says what a press does; for an idea that has been tested already it says so, and queueing it tests it again. Beside a queued idea a tag says its place: next pass for the first, queued · 2, queued · 3 and so on for the others.

    The filter's last segment, after Queued and a thin divider, acts on the whole queue: a button, not a filter. While nothing is queued it is Queue N (in blue), which queues every untested idea, best-scored first, as the list shows them; N counts the whole notebook, not only the ideas shown. Once something is queued it is Clear, which takes every idea off the queue. With nothing queued and nothing untested, there is neither. On a phone the filter and this button scroll sideways together. A running loop reads the queue at its next pass.

    With the ideas cleanup on, an idea merged into another is folded under it ("merged into #3"), its evidence counted with the kept idea's.

    An idea a project's knowledge brought (each accepted entry of a project the loop is linked into becomes one idea) carries a small tag after its title, "from silicon@hw-lab": a link to the project's Knowledge tab, the entry's headline on hover. It is scored and queued like the others. When its entry is retired, it is marked for deletion by "project silicon@hw-lab", unless a measured design keeps it.

    An idea you judge wrong, bad or not useful is deleted with the bin at the end of its row (the loop's owner and its editors; a viewer has none). After you confirm, the loop's agents don't see it again: it is gone from flux loop ideas and from the queue. Nothing offers it again, and the list no longer shows it. Undo in the notice brings it back, and so does Restore on its row under the Deleted filter, which also says who deleted it. The audit names who did it. The idea's text and evaluations stay on record, and a design made from it still names it. A running loop picks up the change at its next read of the notebook. To delete an idea from a terminal, run flux loop ideas --delete N; --restore N brings it back.

    A row that folds several ideas (its ×N: the other ideas of its title and those merged into them) is deleted whole: the confirm says Delete N ideas, each one is deleted as a single idea is (one audit line each, its evaluations kept), and Undo or Restore brings back the ideas deleted with the row. Open the row (press its ×N) to delete one of the folded ideas alone; the terminal's --delete N also deletes that one idea only.

    The loop's agents cannot delete an idea, but they may mark one that is wrong, a duplicate or useless for deletion, with a reason. A person decides, much as a project's curators review proposed knowledge. A marked idea shows a Marked for deletion notice under its row: the reason, which agent marked it and when. Until a person decides, the loop reads the idea as before. The loop's owner and its editors see two buttons on the notice (a viewer sees the notice without them):

    • Accept (the check) deletes the idea, exactly as the bin does: the audit names you and the agent that marked it, and Undo in the notice brings the idea back.
    • Reject (the cross) keeps the idea. The row then says who kept it, and the agents read that a person kept it. The same agent cannot mark it again until the idea has a new evaluation.

    An idea has at most one mark waiting. Deleting a marked idea with the bin, or merging it into another idea, ends the mark. While any mark waits, the filter bar has Marked N, which lists those ideas. Chat's agent proposes deleting an idea as one of its proposals instead, and your Accept in Chat deletes it.

    Elsewhere¶

    • Overview shows the decision against the limits, the best three designs, and a best-so-far chart. Its cards are chosen under Account › Overview layout.
    • flux report RECORD writes the same results as an HTML report beside the record.
