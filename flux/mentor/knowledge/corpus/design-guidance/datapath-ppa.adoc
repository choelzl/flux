= Datapath PPA guidance: parallelism, precision, pipelining

== Spatial parallelism trades area for latency near-linearly — measured here

For a reduction-style datapath (multiply-accumulate over a long dimension), doubling the lane count halves the cycle count while roughly doubling datapath area — and unlike most entries in this corpus, this direction has been measured IN THIS REPOSITORY on ASAP7 with real tools: int8 MAC datapaths at 8/16/32 lanes placed at ~401/~807/~1618 um^2 (OpenROAD, docs/decisions.md D225/D237) with Verilator-measured cycles halving per doubling (docs/decisions.md D231/D236, exact pins in the integration suites). Scope of that measurement: one datapath family, one technology, int8 precision, widths 8-32 — the near-2x-per-doubling shape is NOT established outside that range, and lane counts must divide the dimension they unroll (the RTL evaluator refuses ragged final groups; see the per-op width lists mechanism, D241, when a chain's ops disagree about legal widths).

Utilization is the standing caveat on parallelism: lanes only pay when the workload keeps them busy. A lane count that does not divide the loop bound leaves the final group partly idle; a chain whose ops differ in size by orders of magnitude wastes area wherever a uniform width over-provisions the small ops — measured in this repo as the per-layer engine-assignment frontier ("spend area where the MACs are", docs/decisions.md D236/D237).

== Precision sizing: carry exactly the bits the computation needs

Datapath width should follow the computation's true precision, not a convenient container width: an int8 multiply needs an 8x8 multiplier with a 16-bit product, and an accumulator over K terms needs exactly product-width + ceil(log2(K)) bits. Carrying int8 operands in 32-bit datapaths synthesizes 32-bit multipliers and overstates area and power — this repository measured exactly that overstatement and fixed its derivation to true-precision ports (docs/decisions.md D225/D228). The same principle in reverse is a correctness rule: intermediates must be WIDER than their operands where the arithmetic grows (a sum of two N-bit values needs N+1 bits before any clamp/compare reads it), or the value silently wraps before the logic that was supposed to handle the overflow — a repeatedly observed LLM-generated-RTL defect class in this repo (docs/decisions.md D235).

Verilog-specific trap with area consequences: expression width in Verilog follows the operands, not the intent, so a product formed from insufficiently sized locals can silently build a much larger multiplier than the data needs (this repo once measured a 19x19 multiplier where 8x8 was intended, docs/decisions.md D225 era). Declare per-value wires at their true widths and let the tools prove the rest.

== Pipelining and frequency: direction only

Inserting pipeline registers cuts combinational path length, raising achievable clock frequency at the cost of register area, clock power, and added cycles of latency; whether throughput improves depends on whether the design is frequency-limited or occupancy-limited. This entry is direction-only guidance — this repository's measured pipeline data point is limited to clocked MAC datapaths through the ASAP7 flow (slack numbers in docs/decisions.md D229) and does not establish a general frequency-vs-stages curve. Quantify per design with the real flow (flux_synthesize_with_asap7 / the OpenROAD evaluator's routed mode for post-route timing).

== Power guidance: activity is the lever

Dynamic power follows switching activity times capacitance times V^2 times frequency; the RTL-level levers are reducing activity (clock gating idle registers, operand isolation so idle functional units do not toggle, avoiding free-running counters/shifters) and reducing switched capacitance (smaller/narrower logic, shorter wires — which is again the precision-sizing entry). Direction-only in this corpus: this repository's power numbers come from OpenROAD's placed-design power report (e.g. 11.1 mW for the 8-lane int8 datapath at 2 ns, docs/decisions.md D225) and no clock-gating comparison has been measured here, so no magnitude for these levers is claimed. Note that power composes across sequentially-active engines by energy, not by adding peak powers — the composed evaluator in this repo deliberately refuses to sum power_w across a time-multiplexed chain for exactly that reason (docs/decisions.md D236).
